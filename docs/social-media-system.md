## 1. Brand Voice Guide

### Tone Descriptors

| Descriptor | What It Means in Practice |
|---|---|
| Approachable | Write like you are talking to a friend who asked for design advice. No gatekeeping. |
| Relatable | Reference real-life moments (Saturday morning tile shopping, builder walkthrough chaos). |
| Positive | Lead with excitement and possibility. Never shame someone's current space. |
| Warm Professional | Knowledgeable without being stiff. Friendly without being flippant. |
| Educational | Teach something in every post. Even a pretty photo should include a why. |

**Tone Inspirations (study these accounts for cadence and energy):**
- Rebecca Roberson (warm expertise, approachable authority)
- @shesthecarpenter / Ashley Quintero (hands-on, real, educational)
- studio1NINE1 (polished but personal, design-forward)

### Caption Structure Template

Every caption follows this three-part framework:

```
HOOK (Line 1 — stop the scroll)
Something specific, surprising, or emotionally resonant.

VALUE (Lines 2-5 — teach, show, or tell)
The educational or storytelling body. Give them something useful.

CTA (Final line — one clear ask)
Save this. Drop a comment. Visit the link. Share with your builder.
```

**Example:**
> That grout color is doing more work than you think.
>
> A warm gray grout on white subway tile gives you a softer, lived-in look. A bright white grout on the same tile reads clean and modern. Same tile. Completely different room.
>
> Save this for your next tile appointment.

### Words and Phrases to Use

- "Here is what I tell my clients..."
- "Let me walk you through this."
- "This is one of my favorite combinations."
- "If you are building in the Hill Country..."
- "The difference between good and great is..."
- "You do not have to spend more. You have to choose smarter."
- "Your builder will thank you for knowing this."
- "Design center tip:"
- "Real talk:"
- "Here is why this works."

### Words and Phrases to Avoid

- "Obsessed" (overused)
- "Literally" (filler)
- "Slay," "vibe check," or Gen-Z slang (not the audience)
- "Hubby" or "wifey"
- "DM me for details" as sole CTA (always give value first)
- Any em dashes (use commas, periods, or restructure)
- Contractions (write "do not" instead of "don't", "here is" instead of "here's")
- "Luxury" without specifics (show, do not label)
- Anything condescending about DIY or budget choices

## 7. Privacy Lockdown Instructions

### Facebook (Name: Ena Dodski)

Goal: Make the personal Facebook profile completely invisible to anyone who is not already a friend.

**Step 1: Profile Privacy**
1. Open Facebook. Tap the menu icon (three lines), then Settings and Privacy, then Settings.
2. Go to "How People Find and Contact You."
3. Set "Who can send you friend requests?" to Friends of Friends.
4. Set "Who can see your friends list?" to Only Me.
5. Set "Who can look you up using the email address you provided?" to Only Me.
6. Set "Who can look you up using the phone number you provided?" to Only Me.
7. Set "Do you want search engines outside of Facebook to link to your profile?" to No.

**Step 2: Post Privacy**
1. Go to Settings, then Privacy.
2. Set "Who can see your future posts?" to Friends.
3. Tap "Limit Past Posts" and confirm. This changes ALL previous public posts to Friends Only.

**Step 3: Profile Lock (fastest method)**
1. Go to your profile page.
2. Tap the three dots under your cover photo.
3. Select "Lock Profile."
4. Confirm. This restricts non-friends from seeing photos, posts, and most profile details.

**Step 4: Tag and Mention Control**
1. Settings, then Profile and Tagging.
2. Set "Who can post on your profile?" to Only Me.
3. Turn on "Review tags people add to your posts before the tags appear on Facebook."
4. Set "Who can see posts you are tagged in on your profile?" to Only Me (or Friends).

**Step 5: Search Visibility**
1. Settings, then Privacy, then "Who can look you up?"
2. Ensure all three options (email, phone, search engines) are set to Only Me or No.

**Step 6: Verify**
1. Open a private/incognito browser window.
2. Search "Ena Dodski Facebook" on Google.
3. Try to visit the profile URL directly.
4. Confirm nothing meaningful is visible.

## 8. Content Intake System Design

### Folder Structure (F: Drive)

```

    Drop_Here\
        Photos\
        Videos\
        Screenshots\
        Voice_Notes\
    Processing\
        To_Edit\

        Posted\
    Assets\
        Brand_Templates\
        Fonts\
        Logos\
        Thumbnail_Templates\
    Archive\
        [YYYY-MM]\
```

### Workflow Script Concept

**Drop_Here** is the only folder Ena touches daily. Anything captured on her phone (photos, quick videos, voice memos) gets dropped here.

**Processing pipeline (manual or semi-automated):**

1. **Intake:** New files land in `Drop_Here/` subfolders.
2. **Sort:** Move files to `Processing/To_Edit/` if they need cropping, color correction, or trimming. Move to `Processing/To_Caption/` if media is ready but needs copy.
3. **Caption:** Write caption using the Hook-Value-CTA template. Save as a text file alongside the media file (same filename, .txt extension).
4. **Bundle:** When a post is complete (media + caption + hashtags), move the bundle to `Ready_for_Review/` in a dated folder.
5. **Approve:** Ena reviews `Ready_for_Review/` during weekly session. Approved items move to `Approved/Scheduled/`.
6. **Schedule:** Upload approved bundles to Metricool during the weekly scheduling session.
7. **Archive:** After posting, move to `Archive/[YYYY-MM]/`.

### Weekly Review Workflow (20-30 minutes)

**When:** Sunday evening or Monday morning.

| Step | Time | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 min | Open `Ready_for_Review/`. Scan all bundled posts for the week. |
| 2 | 10 min | Read captions. Check for voice, accuracy, and CTA clarity. Approve or flag. |
| 3 | 5 min | Open Metricool. Upload and schedule approved posts for the week. |
| 4 | 5 min | Glance at last week's analytics. Note top performer. Move posted content to Archive. |
| 5 | 2 min | Check `Drop_Here/` for anything that needs processing before next week. |
